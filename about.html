About the project

Welcome to the Last Gift Project

A program for starting conversations about green burial and end-of-life choices.

Hi! We are a team of students at UC San Diego who created this program as part of SYN 100, a course on environmental challenges and planetary boundaries. While researching death care, we were surprised to learn how much environmental harm comes from conventional burial and cremation and how few people know that greener options exist. We created this toolkit so that any organization can help young adults and seniors learn about their choices and start meaningful conversations with their families.

Why this project exists

Most people never think about what happens to their body after death. When the time comes, families often have only a few days to decide, so they choose the default: conventional burial or cremation. Both have real environmental costs, from embalming chemicals and concrete vaults to the fuel burned during cremation.

Greener options exist. The greenest is natural (green) burial: no embalming, and a biodegradable casket or shroud that returns to the earth. Other alternatives, such as human composting and water cremation, can also reduce environmental impact. Yet many people don't know they have a choice, and funeral homes often offer these options only when families ask. This program helps change that.

Who it's for

Young adults & seniors

This program is designed for young adults (at UCSD) and seniors from retirement homes. You may not be planning your own funeral, but one day you may help a parent or grandparent make these decisions. Knowing your options early, and talking about them with your family, makes those decisions easier and more meaningful.

What participants will gain

By the end of the session

  • Understand the environmental impact of conventional burial and cremation
  • Learn about green alternatives available in California
  • Feel more comfortable starting a conversation about end-of-life wishes with family

The problem

The hidden cost of the default

Into the ground

Conventional burial puts embalming chemicals, concrete, and metal into the ground every year.

Into the air

Cremation uses a lot of fuel and releases emissions, including mercury.

Not required

Embalming and burial vaults are usually not required by law. They became common practice over time.

No time to choose

Families often decide within a few days, so they choose the default.

Greener options

One powerful thing you can do: ask

Most people go through a funeral home, and many funeral homes can offer greener options. But they often only do it when families ask.

The greenest option

Natural (green) burial

  • No embalming fluid
  • A biodegradable casket or shroud (for example, wood, bamboo, or paper)
  • No concrete vault, so the body returns naturally to the earth
  • Families can make it personal by helping carry the casket, filling the grave, or placing handwritten notes

Human composting

Natural organic reduction: the body becomes soil over several weeks. Legal in California starting January 1, 2027.

Water cremation

Alkaline hydrolysis: uses water and an alkaline solution instead of flame.

Greener cremation

Some crematories use filters and scrubbers to reduce emissions, and some in Europe capture the heat for energy.

Biodegradable urns

Made from materials like paper, bamboo, or salt.

"Your choices matter, because when more people ask funeral homes for greener options, more funeral homes start offering them."
