Step 01 Ready

Facilitator Guide

Session plan, script, and FAQ.

Open folder 01 in Drive ↗

This guide walks you through everything you need to run a 45-minute session of The Last Gift to Earth. You don't need to be an expert on death care. Your role is to share information, guide the activity, and create a safe space for conversation. You can make your edits and get started!

Session plan & script

The 45-minute session

Click each part to see the script and key points. The script is a guide that covers all the key points you should introduce during the talk.

  1. 0–5 min
    Welcome & introductionSlides 1–2 · Make people feel safe and comfortable, introduce the topic gently
    "Welcome, and thank you for being here! Today we're talking about something most of us avoid: what happens to our bodies after we die? This isn't about anyone dying soon. It's about knowing your options and how those choices affect the planet."
  2. 5–15 min
    The problemSlides 3–4 · Show the environmental cost of conventional options

    Key points to cover

    • Conventional burial puts embalming chemicals, concrete, and metal into the ground every year.
    • Cremation uses a lot of fuel and releases emissions, including mercury.
    • Embalming and burial vaults are usually not required by law. They became common practice over time.
    • Families often decide within a few days, so they choose the default.
  3. 15–25 min
    The optionsSlides 5–6 · Introduce greener alternatives
    "Worth noticing: most people go through a funeral home, and many funeral homes can offer greener options. But they often only do it when families ask. So one of the most powerful things you can do is simply ask."

    The greenest option

    • No embalming fluid
    • A biodegradable casket or shroud (for example, wood, bamboo, or paper)
    • No concrete vault, so the body returns naturally to the earth
    • Families can make it personal by helping carry the casket, filling the grave, or placing handwritten notes

    Greener alternatives

    • Human composting (natural organic reduction): the body becomes soil over several weeks; legal in California starting January 1, 2027
    • Water cremation (alkaline hydrolysis): uses water and an alkaline solution instead of flame
    • Greener cremation: some crematories use filters and scrubbers to reduce emissions, and some in Europe capture the heat for energy
    • Biodegradable urns: made from materials like paper, bamboo, or salt
  4. 25–35 min
    ActivityMake the information hands-on and memorable

    Activity instructions coming soon. See Step 03: Activity.

  5. 35–40 min
    Conversation practiceSlides 7–8 · Build confidence to talk with family

    Hand out the Family Conversation Guide. In pairs, participants practice one opening line, for example:

    "I learned something interesting today about green burial. Have you ever thought about what you'd want?"
  6. 40–45 min
    Wrap upSlide 9 · Share resources and collect feedback
    "Your choices matter, because when more people ask funeral homes for greener options, more funeral homes start offering them."

    Key points to cover

    • Display the post-survey QR code
    • Share the Instagram handle: @syn_100_team_7
    • Thank everyone for coming

About the greener options

What is green burial?

A burial without embalming chemicals, concrete vaults, or metal caskets. The body is placed in a biodegradable casket or shroud so it can return naturally to the earth.
